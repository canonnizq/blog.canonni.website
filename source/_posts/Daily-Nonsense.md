## Language Arts (2023-12-23)

(inspired by a conversation between me and `[x]`)

My `[x]` once said to me, "Chinese classes were never about the language; they were about memory. If you want to learn Chinese, go outside and talk instead of reading words on books. Words are dead. Languages are alive."

When I told `[x]` about this, he said we needed more teachers like mine. But here's the truth: she never said anything like that. I made everything up.

What's my point? Language arts classes do not help students understand a language more comprehensively. They help students improve their memory by forcibly throwing words at them.

Think about the last language arts test you took. How much of the test was actually about utilizing the language? How much of the test was about expressing yourself?

For me personally, the answer is infinitely close to zero. Every part of the tests are read, referred to a rubric, and graded. Even essays. Everything is structured and manufactured. Nothing is truly unique.

Now, where and when was the last time you expressed your true thoughts? I'm sure it was not in a language arts class. If it was, I'm impressed you had the time and motivation to do so after half an hour of your teacher playing a presentation filled with words to memorize.

Now, I want you to think of a simple question: Why am I learning this?

Have an answer? Good. Forget it. The only reason you're learning is for good-looking grades and greater chances of a bright future. If you inspire to become an author or poet, save your ideas for somewhere else. In school, your only goal is to obey and submit.
